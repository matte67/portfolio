## Connections should make things simpler

Boccone is about recipes; Spesino is about offers and groceries. When products share an account, moving between them can raise questions: where am I signing in? Which account am I using? How do I return to the product?

Nodale makes that transition recognisable. It is the common identity and account portal supporting access to the two products.

## Put the destination first

The message is brief: “One account. Your apps.” Boccone and Spesino are visible with their own names and logos. The portal lets people choose where to continue and provides access to account management.

The hierarchy shortens the distance between intention and action: recognise the product, choose the destination, continue. The common identity stays present without competing with the applications.

## A restrained identity

I chose a lowercase wordmark, a warm palette, and a composition built around typography and space. The final dot relates to Boccone, while each product retains its own colours and distinctive marks.

The work also involved logo proportions and optical alignment. A shared system needs to accommodate different identities while giving each a clear place.

## The boundary between account and product

Brand content and product destinations are configuration, separate from portal logic. The interface guides the person; sign-in flows and applications keep dedicated responsibilities.

This separation lets copy, assets, and links change without rebuilding the page around one particular product.

## The outcome

The portal is public and accessible from the Boccone and Spesino ecosystem. This case study covers identity and the account experience; it makes no claims about additional integrations or unmeasured performance.
