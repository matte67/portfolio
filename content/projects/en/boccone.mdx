## What should I cook tonight?

One recipe saved in a chat, another in bookmarks, a few ingredients left in the fridge. Inspiration comes from many places; turning it into a meal still means finding and organising the information.

Boccone gives recipes a useful, recognisable home: a personal space to collect them and a community to explore when you need an idea.

## From discovery to cooking

Search by recipe, ingredient, or tag works alongside categories. Available ingredients provide another starting point for finding something you can prepare. Recipe details bring quantities, servings, timing, and instructions together; cooking mode guides preparation step by step.

## An identity that invites you to cook

I designed a warm interface with natural colours, illustrations, and food photography. Categories support discovery, while consistent cards make different recipes easier to compare. On mobile, navigation and content reorganise to give reading more room.

Personal and public spaces serve different purposes: keeping your own recipes or sharing a preparation with the community. Public profiles give recipes their author context.

## Behind the interface

I worked on identity, user experience, and development. Ingredient and quantity rules are separate from use cases, persistence, and UI components. The local cookbook uses IndexedDB; the public experience runs through the application and its Cloudflare infrastructure.

These boundaries let search, publishing, and cooking mode evolve without concentrating every responsibility in the recipe page.

## A product within an ecosystem

Boccone is available online. Personal services use the Nodale account portal; Spesino is the dedicated grocery and offers product. Each keeps its own identity and experience.
