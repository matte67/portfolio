## Give a good recipe somewhere to go

A recipe discovered today should be easy to find tomorrow, adjust to the people at the table, and share with someone who enjoys cooking. Boccone connects those moments: a personal cookbook, new ideas to explore, and a community where the people behind the dishes are recognisable.

The goal is to make everyday cooking easier and more participatory. The product should be useful both to someone looking for dinner inspiration and to someone building and publishing their own cookbook.

## Create a recipe in your own cooking style

A familiar dish can become a complete recipe: cover photo, ingredients, quantities, servings, timing, and difficulty. Instructions are organised into steps, each with space for images and useful references. A faster variation can live alongside the main preparation.

Creating a recipe starts with preserving something of your own. Publishing it to the community is a separate choice, making the preparation available to others with its author context intact.

## Search for a dish or start with what you have

The community supports search by recipe name, ingredient, or tag, alongside category browsing and available-ingredient matching. These are different starting points for the same question: what can I cook?

Cards bring photographs, descriptions, origins, and authors together. The detail view connects servings, ingredients, and instructions, so a recipe can move from inspiration to preparation without reconstructing its information elsewhere.

<ProjectFigure src="/media/boccone/community.webp" alt="Boccone community with search, category filters, and recipes published by the official profile." caption="Recipe discovery: search and filters support a recognisable photographic catalogue." />

## A community of cooks, too

The Cooks view shifts attention from dishes to people. Each public profile brings together an introduction and published recipes. Finding an interesting dish can become a way to explore its author’s cookbook.

This is a central goal of Boccone: make a cooking style recognisable and give an author’s contributions continuity. A recipe becomes an entry point to more ideas from the same kitchen.

<ProjectFigure src="/media/boccone/cooks.webp" alt="Boccone Cooks view showing public profiles, introductions, and recipe counts." caption="From recipes to their authors: the community can also be explored through its cooks." />

## A cookbook for the moment you cook

After choosing a dish, users can adjust servings and check which ingredients are already available. Cooking mode presents one step at a time, with images, ingredients, and progress, keeping the recipe readable during preparation.

I gave this moment a different hierarchy from the catalogue: fewer things to compare, more space for the next action. Discovering a recipe and following it at the stove need different kinds of attention.

<ProjectFigure src="/media/boccone/cooking.webp" alt="Boccone cooking mode on the first step of pumpkin and Taleggio risotto, showing servings and a preparation photograph." caption="Cooking mode focuses attention on the step being prepared." />

## Missing ingredients become a grocery list

Before cooking, users can mark ingredients they already have and create a list of what is missing. That list remains usable in Boccone: quantities can be reviewed, products added, and purchases checked off.

For finding offers and organising a route, the next step is Spesino. The choice is explicit: a straightforward list for someone who needs it immediately, or a grocery comparison for someone deciding where to shop.

<ProjectFigure src="/media/boccone/shopping-choice.webp" alt="Boccone offers a choice between creating the list alone or finding offers and a route with Spesino." caption="The connection to Spesino starts with a concrete need: buying what the recipe requires." />

## Why Boccone belongs to Nodale

Boccone and [Spesino](/en/work/spesino) address neighbouring moments in everyday life: deciding what to prepare and organising what to buy. Nodale is their common identity, with a shared account entry point and products that retain recognisable purposes and visual languages.

Integration is also about continuity. Turning recipe ingredients into a list should not mean entering everything again. Boccone preserves the cooking context; Spesino adds product, offer, supermarket, and travel comparisons.

## Growing the community, one publication at a time

The launch strategy centres on the official **@boccone** profile: consistently publish complete, carefully prepared recipes so visitors immediately find something to try. Clear ingredients, preparation photographs, and editorial sources also demonstrate the kind of contribution the platform wants to welcome.

The official profile identifies the project’s own content. The goal is to expand the catalogue, give people reasons to return, and invite other cooks to publish their recipes. This is a strategy for editorial growth and participation; its outcomes still need to be measured in use.

## Design and development

I worked on identity, interface design, and full-stack development. Recipe, ingredient, and quantity rules are separate from use cases, persistence adapters, and components. The local cookbook and community services have dedicated boundaries; the public experience uses React, Vinext, and Cloudflare infrastructure.

The same approach shapes the connection to Spesino: share a journey and selected components while keeping each product’s responsibilities clear.
