## Grocery shopping is a sequence of decisions

Which product, package size, promotion, and supermarket should you choose? A cheaper package may contain less; an extra stop can absorb the benefit of an offer. Spesino brings those decisions back to their starting point: what the list actually needs.

The goal is an understandable shopping plan in which quantities, alternatives, and travel can be considered together.

## A category or a specific product

“Pasta” and a particular package of pasta are different requests. Search can start with a generic category or a specific product, followed by quantity and preference confirmation. The list collects those needs before comparison begins.

The summary stays beside search: adding a product immediately shows how the list changes while preserving the requests already entered.

## Decide where to shop

The journey continues through supermarkets, travel, and preferences. Selecting stores makes the comparison’s scope explicit: which locations you are willing to visit and which journey is worth considering.

These decisions ground the proposal in a shopping context. Product price is one part of the choice, alongside distance, time, and the number of stops.

<ProjectFigure src="/media/spesino/supermarkets.webp" alt="Spesino supermarket selection opened from the Boccone shopping workflow." caption="The supermarket step retains a connection to the original Boccone list." />

## Understand a proposal and compare an alternative

The plan distinguishes requested quantities from the packages needed to fulfil them. Formats, surplus quantities, promotional conditions, and uncovered requests must remain visible.

Alternatives allow a decision to be revisited: users can examine another offer and see which request it addresses. A useful result explains why it was proposed and allows it to change.

<ProjectFigure src="/media/spesino/comparison.webp" alt="Development example comparing alternative apple offers in Spesino." caption="Development comparison screen: alternatives remain connected to the list request." />

## Product cost and the value of the journey

Results compare product costs and, when data and preferences allow, the trade-off with travel. Choosing one supermarket can be reasonable even when a lower-price combination exists.

Covered and missing quantities remain distinct. Calculation limits need to be visible, especially when the system cannot compare every alternative or estimate a route.

## When the list starts with a recipe

With [Boccone](/en/work/boccone), the starting point can be a dish. Missing ingredients become a list that can be used immediately; people who want offer and route comparisons can continue with Spesino.

That continuity is deliberate: from deciding what to cook to deciding what to buy. A recipe should not become another generic search that forces someone to start again.

## Two products, one family

Spesino belongs to Nodale alongside Boccone. A shared account entry point makes the transition recognisable, while separate products retain their own jobs: recipes and community on one side, grocery lists and comparison on the other.

The experience also shares selected list components. Keeping these consistent makes actions familiar without introducing uncalculated prices or results into a simple checklist.

## Design and product status

I worked on user experience, architecture, and full-stack development. The domain distinguishes requests, products, packages, and promotions; use cases coordinate comparison; adapters handle catalogues, data, and external services. Next.js and React compose the interface.

Spesino continues to evolve as a public prototype. Images show development examples; catalogue availability, offer validity, and routes depend on available sources and services. Its design goal is an understandable decision; actual savings need to be evaluated against real purchases.
